Sam 169.06 vs 162.96 Jon
GAME OF THE WEEK

Our opening Game of the Week delivered immediately, with Sam squeezing past Jon by just 6.10 points. Jon appeared to hold the stronger hand after Caleb Williams produced the week’s highest fantasy score, an outrageous 42.06, in Chicago’s 59–37 demolition of Carolina. Amon-Ra St. Brown added 28.70 in Detroit’s overtime thriller and Zay Flowers contributed 28.00 as Baltimore dismantled Indianapolis. Those three combined for 98.76 points, but Jon’s lineup became decidedly mortal elsewhere — Marvin Harrison Jr., Kenny Gainwell and Quentin Johnston managed only 13.50 points combined, leaving the door ajar. Sam duly walked through it. Chase Brown, Ladd McConkey, Mike Evans, Deebo Samuel and Pittsburgh’s defence all scored at least 16 points, but the decisive intervention came from Isaiah Likely, whose 29.80 under the Sunday-night lights erased much of Caleb’s earlier advantage. Jon supplied the week’s biggest explosion; Sam supplied the better team.

Dan 130.14 vs 82.66 Ollie

Dan began the season with a comfortable 47.48-point victory over Ollie, primarily because of a brutal mismatch at wide receiver. Christian Watson scored 33.70, Jameson Williams added 25.40 and CeeDee Lamb contributed 15.50, giving Dan 74.60 points from three receiving positions. Ollie’s Drake London, Keenan Allen and Courtland Sutton answered with only 17.90 combined. Watson’s 147 receiving yards made him one of the few bright spots in Green Bay’s defeat to Minnesota, while Williams benefited from Detroit’s 31–30 overtime shootout against New Orleans. Ollie received reasonable scores from Justin Herbert and De’Von Achane, but no starter reached 15 points and the lineup lacked the explosive performance required to reopen the contest. Dan even survived Jordan Addison’s zero and modest returns from Omarion Hampton and Harold Fannin Jr. Dan starts 1-0; Ollie’s Tim Riggins tribute showed considerably less fight than its namesake.

Ed 158.65 vs 96.77 Sergio

Ed swept Sergio aside by 61.88 points, powered by three performances above 20. Bijan Robinson scored 33.40, combining 83 rushing yards with 90 receiving yards and a touchdown despite Atlanta losing in Pittsburgh. Chris Olave added 30.20 after catching ten passes for 182 yards in New Orleans’ overtime defeat to Detroit, while Kyle Monangai contributed 22.40 during Chicago’s record-breaking afternoon in Carolina. Sergio briefly had something to cling to through Jalen Hurts’ 25.62, but Christian McCaffrey was limited to 14.80 and the supporting cast offered very little — Terry McLaurin, Jake Ferguson, Dylan Sampson and the Chargers defence combined for only 14.40 points. Ed was already well positioned through Sunday’s early games and Stafford’s underwhelming 8.30 never became remotely costly. Ed opens with an emphatic win; Sergio has immediate reconstruction work to undertake.

Andy 139.55 vs 155.60 Matt

Andy and Matt produced a competitive matchup eventually settled by two enormous Sunday performances. Ashton Jeanty gave Andy 35.00 points as Las Vegas defeated Miami, while Bucky Irving added 21.10 and Dak Prescott contributed 19.60. Matt responded through Kenneth Walker’s 37.40 during Kansas City’s Monday-night victory over Denver and Justin Jefferson’s 31.20 against Green Bay. Walker rushed for 173 yards and a touchdown, making Monday Night Football the decisive final act rather than a ceremonial procession. Andy held a plausible route to victory after Sunday, but Walker needed only a productive evening to overhaul the remaining deficit — and duly delivered the week’s leading rushing performance. Matt won despite carrying zeroes from D. Stirling and Kyle Pitts, while Andy was undermined by quieter returns from Puka Nacua, MarShawn Lloyd, Michael Pittman and Kayshon Boutte. One Kenneth Walker masterclass can apparently conceal a multitude of lineup sins.

Jack 157.26 vs 82.32 Ben
BLOWOUT OF THE WEEK

Jack delivered Week 1’s most comprehensive beating, defeating Ben by 74.94 points. Jonathan Taylor scored 27.00, Dallas Goedert supplied 25.70, and D’Andre Swift erupted for 34.20 as Chicago’s rushing attack overwhelmed Carolina — Swift ran for 124 yards and three touchdowns as part of a 59-point outburst. Those three starters alone amassed 86.90 points, more than Ben’s entire lineup. Chuba Hubbard’s 24.70 and Jeremiyah Love’s 14.10 provided isolated resistance, but Drake Maye threw three interceptions, Ja’Marr Chase scored only 3.20 and Colston Loveland recorded a goose egg. The tight-end battle was particularly gruesome: Goedert outscored Loveland 25.70 to 0.00. Jack carried a few weak scores himself, including 2.20 from Travis Hunter and 2.50 from the Rams defence, but Ben never came close to exploiting them. By Sunday evening this had ceased being a contest and become an item of paperwork.

Harry 109.06 vs 121.57 Sven
BUMFIGHT OF THE WEEK

Harry and Sven claim the season’s first Bumfight of the Week, producing the lowest average score outside the blowout at just 115.32. Harry received a monstrous 40.66 from Josh Allen, who threw for 334 yards and accounted for three touchdowns as Buffalo escaped Houston 36–31 — over 37% of Harry’s entire score. Cam Skattebo added 15.90, but no other scoring starter reached 13 points, while Brock Bowers contributed nothing. Sven somehow contrived to win despite Kyler Murray scoring a catastrophic 1.22. David Montgomery’s 30.90, DJ Moore’s 22.00 and Jacksonville’s 18-point defensive return against Cleveland instead dragged Sven across the line. This was less a triumph than a controlled stumble towards safety, but Sven will happily take the 1-0 record. Harry, meanwhile, wasted the week’s second-highest individual fantasy score.

Boz 139.16 vs 183.55 Rommel

Rommel posted the week’s highest team score, beating Boz by 44.39 points behind a lineup containing four major performances. Derrick Henry led the way with 37.70, rushing for 144 yards and three touchdowns as Baltimore defeated Indianapolis 41–23. Jalen Coker added 34.80 during Carolina and Chicago’s extraordinary 96-point shootout, while Jaxson Dart scored 31.30 under the Sunday-night lights and Trey McBride supplied 26.75. Boz had started promisingly — Jaxon Smith-Njigba scored 27.20 in Wednesday’s opener and Lamar Jackson added 30.46 — but too many teammates settled into single figures. Quinshon Judkins, D.K. Metcalf, Josh Downs and Cade Otton combined for only 28.25 points. Rommel could therefore absorb Romeo Doubs’ zero without consequence and steadily accelerated through the Sunday windows before Dart finished the job. Boz’s 139.16 would have been competitive elsewhere; against Rommel’s buzz saw, it was not enough.

Mike 139.21 vs 125.09 James

Mike secured a 14.12-point victory over James in a matchup defined by one enormous running-back performance and a punishing quarterback disparity. Patrick Mahomes scored 23.96, while Jahmyr Gibbs produced 36.50 after rushing for 156 yards and two touchdowns in Detroit’s overtime win over New Orleans. James responded through Nico Collins’ 21.30, R.J. Harvey’s 19.50 and the Raiders defence’s 18.50, keeping the contest alive into Monday Night Football. James needed Bo Nix to overturn the deficit against a Kansas City defence that dominated Denver — Nix finished with only 4.94, while Mahomes comfortably protected Mike’s lead. That 19.02-point quarterback gap was larger than the final margin. Mike survived a miserable 1.20 from Jaylen Waddle because Gibbs had already provided the matchup’s decisive foundation. James reached Monday with a route to victory, but the requested miracle ran into Mahomes, Kansas City, and the regrettable inconvenience of reality.
